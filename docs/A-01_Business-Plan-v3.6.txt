MISSION & VISION
Mission Statement
Quez Coffee Co. exists to serve exceptional honey craft coffee — sourced from local Iowa wildflower honey, built on veteran values, and rooted in the communities we call home. Every cup, every location, and every product we put our name on reflects the same standard: quality without compromise, service without excuses, and a brand story no corporate chain can replicate.
Vision Statement
Quez Coffee Co. is built to become more than a mobile craft coffee bar. The vision is a full-scale American coffee brand — beginning with a single drive-thru and walk-up unit in Council Bluffs, Iowa and growing into a diversified portfolio that includes Quez Coffee cafés, hybrid café and drive-thru locations, mobile catering operations, and a packaged retail line of bottled and canned honey craft beverages available in grocery stores, gas stations, and major retail outlets nationwide.
The growth path is deliberate: prove the concept locally, build the brand regionally, scale through veteran franchisees, and take the Iowa wildflower honey story to a national audience. What starts at a drive-thru window in Council Bluffs is the foundation of something that belongs on shelves across the country.
Every format — mobile craft coffee bar, café, catering, or can — carries the same identity: veteran-owned, honey-crafted, community-rooted. That never changes regardless of how large the brand grows.
SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Quez Coffee Co. LLC is a veteran-owned, drive-thru mobile coffee operation seeking $55,000 in SBA Veterans Advantage financing to launch in Council Bluffs, Iowa in February 2027. Combined with a $10,000 owner equity injection (15.4% of total project cost), the total startup capitalization is $65,000. SECTION 2 — FOUNDER BACKGROUND & VETERAN QUALIFICATION
2.1 Military Service
Ryan Joseph Rodriquez is an active U.S. Army Reserve E-6 (Staff Sergeant) with 12 years of creditable service, separating from active duty on March 10, 2027 (ETS date). His primary Military Occupational Specialty (MOS) is 91B — Wheeled Vehicle Mechanic — a technical certification that directly translates to the owner-build construction model at the core of Quez Coffee Co.’s low-cost launch strategy. As a certified mechanic with both a professional mechanic’s tool kit and a full construction toolkit, the owner possesses the equipment and expertise to build, modify, and maintain the mobile craft coffee bar without contractor labor for structural and fabrication work. This capability reduces construction costs by an estimated $15,000–$20,000 compared to purchasing a purpose-built unit from a commercial fabricator.
His secondary MOS is 56M — Religious Affairs Specialist — a role requiring interpersonal leadership, cross-cultural communication, and the ability to build trust with diverse communities under high-pressure conditions. This skillset is directly applicable to building the customer relationships, community presence, and local brand loyalty that drive repeat business in a neighborhood coffee operation.
Together, these MOS designations represent a founder who can build and maintain his own equipment at minimal labor cost while simultaneously creating the community connections that convert first-time customers into daily regulars. Both are material competitive advantages that no hired management team can replicate.
Prior to launch, the owner managed coffee service operations for the U.S. Army chapel program for six months, overseeing daily beverage service for 50–100 personnel. Equipment operated included commercial drip brewers and espresso machines. Responsibilities encompassed equipment maintenance, sanitation compliance, inventory management, and service flow under high-demand conditions. This experience provides direct, hands-on operational familiarity with the equipment, cleanliness standards, and throughput management that define daily mobile craft coffee bar operations — filling the food service experience gap that first-time operators typically carry into launch.
2.2 Transition & Launch Timeline
The transition from active duty to business ownership is deliberately structured to eliminate any income gap. Transitional leave pay continues through the ETS date of March 10, 2027. The mobile craft coffee bar soft opens in February 2027 — while transitional pay is still active — meaning the business begins generating revenue before military income ends. Following ETS, the owner's income base consists of VA disability compensation (estimated $2,000/month at 90% rating) and U.S. Army Reserves drill pay. TRICARE Reserve Select, available through continued Reserves participation, covers full family healthcare at $287/month — significantly below market cost for equivalent private coverage. This combined post-ETS income structure is sufficient to cover personal living expenses without requiring the business to generate owner compensation in its first operating months, a material advantage for cash flow and debt service in the early period.

SECTION 3 — BUSINESS CONCEPT & DIFFERENTIATION
3.1 The Concept
Quez Coffee Co. LLC operates as a mobile craft coffee bar — a custom-built, fully equipped commercial unit designed as a professional drive-thru and walk-up service bar — deployed at high-traffic locations across the Council Bluffs metro on a rotating schedule. The operating model mirrors the origins of Scooter’s Coffee — founded in Bellevue, Nebraska in 1998 with a single drive-thru kiosk, now operating over 700 locations — and Dutch Bros Coffee, which built a national brand from the same format. Both companies validate that the drive-thru mobile coffee model is one of the most scalable and profitable in the food service industry.
The distinction for Quez Coffee Co. is product differentiation. Where Scooter's, Dutch Bros, and Starbucks offer standardized national menus, Quez Coffee Co. builds its identity around a proprietary line of Iowa wildflower honey craft drinks — a concept that cannot be replicated by corporate competitors because it is rooted in local sourcing, a local veteran story, and a genuinely unique menu developed specifically for this market.
3.2 Market Position in Council Bluffs
The primary competitive differentiator for Quez Coffee Co. is the product, not the format. Iowa wildflower honey craft coffee — sourced from local Iowa producers, built into a proprietary 15-drink menu, and owned entirely by Quez Coffee Co. LLC — does not exist anywhere in the Council Bluffs or Omaha metro market. No competitor, fixed or mobile, carries a honey-craft identity tied to a verified local sourcing story. That is the moat. The mobile craft coffee bar format is the delivery mechanism that brings that product directly to the customer.
The Council Bluffs drive-thru coffee segment includes five operators: three Starbucks locations (3825 Denmark Drive, 2550 West Broadway, and inside Target at 3804 Metro Drive); two Scooter’s Coffee drive-thrus (1738 Madison Avenue and 3030 West Broadway); Frosted Pine Coffee, a veteran-owned two-window drive-thru kiosk at 2701 East Kanesville Boulevard averaging 190 cars per day since opening in September 2024; and Louie’s Coffee House, a family-owned café with drive-thru that opened January 2026 at 928 Valley View Drive. None of these operators are mobile. None rotate locations. None source Iowa wildflower honey. Quez Coffee Co. is the only operator in this market with all three.
3.3 The Honey Craft Differentiator & Iowa Sourcing
The core menu centers on a proprietary line of honey-based craft coffee drinks using Iowa wildflower honey sourced from local Iowa producers. This sourcing decision is both a brand story and a supply chain strategy. Two honey suppliers are maintained at all times, eliminating single-source failure risk. The Iowa honey provenance story — authentic, community-rooted, and directly tied to the founder's commitment to local business — is a marketing asset that corporate competitors structurally cannot replicate regardless of budget. National chains source commoditized ingredients. Quez Coffee Co. sources from neighbors.
All 15 drink recipes are formally owned by Quez Coffee Co. LLC, documented in a completed Recipe Bible, costed at the ingredient level, and standardized for consistent execution regardless of who is behind the counter. A laminated recipe card system is in place for back-of-house reference.
3.4 Operating Model & Rotation Schedule
Iowa Administrative Code 481-31 governs mobile food unit licensing and restricts extended stationary operation at any single location. Quez Coffee Co. operates a deliberate 2–3 location rotation across Council Bluffs high-traffic sites, deploying to each site on a recurring weekly schedule. This rotation model turns a regulatory requirement into a marketing advantage — multiple locations mean multiple customer touchpoints, greater brand visibility across the city, and reduced dependency on any single site's traffic volume.
Target operating hours: Monday through Friday 6:00 AM – 2:00 PM, Saturday 7:00 AM – 3:00 PM, Sunday 7:00 AM – 1:00 PM. These hours are designed to capture the two peak specialty coffee traffic windows: the morning commuter rush (6–9 AM, approximately 40% of daily volume) and the mid-morning to early afternoon secondary peak (9 AM – 2 PM).

SECTION 4 — MARKET ANALYSIS
4.1 Market Overview
The Omaha/Council Bluffs Consolidated Metropolitan Statistical Area has a population of approximately 970,000 residents, with strong cross-river commuter patterns via the I-80 and I-29 corridors. Council Bluffs has a population of approximately 62,600, a median household income of $64,578, and a median age of 38.7 years. Adults aged 25–44 — the highest-spending specialty coffee cohort — represent 26.4% of the population with a median household income of $72,218. The city sits at the convergence of two major interstate systems, generating commuter traffic volumes that significantly exceed what the local population alone would produce. The average Council Bluffs household owns two vehicles and commutes an average of 17.9 minutes — a car-dependent commuter pattern structurally built for drive-thru coffee.
The U.S. specialty coffee market exceeds $47 billion annually and is growing at approximately 7% per year. Drive-thru coffee has outpaced sit-down coffee shop growth for five consecutive years, driven by consumer preference for speed and convenience. Drive-thru operations generate 70–80% of revenue for leading chains, validating the format Quez Coffee Co. has adopted. Nationally, specialty coffee consumption has reached a 14-year high, with 64% of adults aged 25–39 consuming specialty coffee weekly (Specialty Coffee Association, 2025). Nearly 60% of American coffee consumers now prefer gourmet or specialty coffee over standard options, and specialty drinkers are 75% more likely to purchase coffee away from home — validating both the product position and the drive-thru delivery format.

SECTION 5 — OPERATIONS PLAN
5.1 Mobile Craft Coffee Bar — Build & Construction Strategy
The mobile craft coffee bar will be owner-built under the direct supervision of Ryan Joseph Rodriquez, utilizing the mechanical expertise developed over 12 years as a U.S. Army 91B Wheeled Vehicle Mechanic. A new or used commercial trailer shell in the $8,000–$10,000 range will be sourced, inspected, and converted into a fully equipped drive-thru and walk-up coffee operation. The owner possesses a complete professional mechanic’s toolkit and construction toolkit — no tool procurement costs are required for the build.
Licensed electrical and plumbing contractors will be engaged for all permit-required work, operating under the owner’s direct supervision. This hybrid approach — owner fabrication plus licensed trade contractors for regulatory compliance items — is estimated to reduce total build cost by $15,000–$20,000 versus a fully contracted commercial build. The unit is being designed to meet Iowa DIAL self-contained mobile food unit requirements, which eliminates the commissary agreement requirement; this classification will be confirmed at plan review prior to construction.
Iowa DIAL requires plan review and approval BEFORE trailer construction begins. Scaled floor drawings, equipment schedule, plumbing schematics, and finish material specifications will be submitted to DIAL Food Plan Review (515.350.7587) prior to any build activity. This is a gating requirement and is built into the pre-launch timeline. SBA loan funds are targeted for November 2026 disbursement, which precedes the build window.

SECTION 6 — MARKETING & BRAND STRATEGY

Quez Coffee Co. positions as a premium yet approachable veteran-owned honey craft coffee brand. The name is personal, memorable, and carries no corporate DNA — it signals that this business was built by a specific person with a specific story, not a committee. The visual identity of black, gold, and cream is disciplined and professional, aligned with the veteran ownership story without being overtly military.
Three brand pillars — veteran-owned, honey-crafted, community-rooted — are authentic and structurally unreplicable by competitors. Every touchpoint reinforces all three.

7.5 Owner Compensation Structure
Year 1 — LLC (no S-Corp election). Owner takes a $2,500/month owner's draw beginning Month 3 (April 2027). This is not a salary — no payroll taxes are withheld at time of draw. Self-employment tax on net profit is paid at year-end. The $2,500/month draw covers the gap between the owner's guaranteed post-ETS personal income (VA disability ~$2,000/month + Reserves net ~$338/month = ~$2,338/month) and monthly personal household obligations.
Year 2 — S-Corp election (IRS Form 2553). Once Base scenario revenue is confirmed, the LLC elects S-Corp status. The owner establishes a $3,000/month reasonable salary subject to payroll taxes, plus distributions from remaining profit. S-Corp election at the Year 2 income level produces meaningful payroll tax savings compared to the LLC/self-employment structure.
The owner's personal living expenses in Year 1 are primarily covered by VA disability (~$2,000/month) and Reserves drill pay net of TRICARE Reserve Select family healthcare premium (~$338/month). TRICARE Reserve Select covers full family healthcare at $287/month — a significant benefit that eliminates market-rate health insurance costs of $800–$1,500/month for equivalent private family coverage. This structure means the business does not need to generate owner compensation in its first two months of operation, materially strengthening early cash flow.
