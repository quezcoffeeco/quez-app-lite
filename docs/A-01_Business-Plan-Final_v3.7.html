MISSION & VISION

Mission. Quez Coffee Co. exists to serve exceptional honey craft coffee — sourced from local Iowa wildflower honey, built on veteran values, and rooted in the communities we call home. Every cup, every location, and every product we put our name on reflects the same standard: quality without compromise, service without excuses, and a brand story no corporate chain can replicate.

Vision. Quez Coffee Co. is built to become more than a mobile craft coffee bar. The vision is a full-scale American coffee brand — beginning with a single drive-thru and walk-up unit in Council Bluffs and growing into a diversified portfolio of Quez cafés, hybrid café/drive-thru locations, mobile catering operations, and a packaged retail line of bottled and canned honey craft beverages on grocery and convenience-store shelves nationwide. The growth path is deliberate: prove the concept locally, build the brand regionally, scale through veteran franchisees, and take the Iowa wildflower honey story to a national audience.

SECTION 3 — BUSINESS CONCEPT & DIFFERENTIATION

3.1 The Concept

Quez Coffee Co. LLC operates as a mobile craft coffee bar — a custom-built, fully equipped commercial unit designed as a professional drive-thru and walk-up service bar — deployed at high-traffic Council Bluffs locations on a rotating schedule. The operating model is structurally identical to the origin model of Scooter's Coffee (Bellevue, Nebraska, 1998 — now 700+ locations) and Dutch Bros Coffee (Grants Pass, Oregon, 1992 — now 900+ locations). Both companies validate that the drive-thru mobile coffee model is one of the most scalable and profitable formats in the food service industry.

The distinction for Quez Coffee Co. is product, not format. Where Scooter's, Dutch Bros, Starbucks, and the incoming 7 Brew offer standardized national menus, Quez Coffee Co. builds its identity around a proprietary line of Iowa wildflower honey craft drinks — a concept that corporate competitors cannot replicate because it is rooted in local sourcing, a local veteran story, and a genuinely unique menu developed specifically for this market.

3.3 The Honey Craft Differentiator & Iowa Sourcing

The core menu centers on a proprietary line of honey-based craft coffee drinks using Iowa wildflower honey sourced from local Iowa producers. This sourcing decision is both a brand story and a supply chain strategy. Two honey suppliers will be maintained at all times to eliminate single-source failure risk. Iowa's market includes 127 certified honey producers across 93 cities, giving Quez Coffee Co. deep supply redundancy and competitive sourcing leverage. Anchor candidates include Ebert Honey (Lynnville, IA — operating since 1980 and producing both wholesale buckets and labeled retail) and Lappe's Bee Supply (West Des Moines — wholesale sizing 2 oz through 60 lb pails). Supplier onboarding follows a documented selection rubric: certified food-safe processing, 60-lb bucket wholesale availability, twice-monthly delivery cadence, $18/lb wholesale ceiling, and capacity to fulfill 8–12 lbs/week at full operating volume. Both suppliers are confirmed and contracts executed before Iowa DIAL plan submission in September 2026, ensuring supply-chain risk is closed before regulatory review begins. The Iowa honey provenance is a marketing asset that corporate competitors structurally cannot replicate regardless of budget — national chains source commoditized ingredients; Quez Coffee Co. sources from neighbors.

All 17 drink recipes — including the seasonal Hot Chocolate and Matcha Latte additions — are formally owned by Quez Coffee Co. LLC, documented in a completed Recipe Bible (F-01 v2.0), costed at the ingredient level, and standardized for consistent execution regardless of who is behind the counter. Recipes are integrated into the proprietary Quez App Lite operations platform, which surfaces build steps, mandatory dissolve warnings, and modifier gating at the point of service — eliminating training drift and ensuring every cup matches spec from cup #1 through cup #10,000.

3.4 Operating Model & Rotation Schedule

Iowa Administrative Code 481-31 governs mobile food unit licensing and restricts extended stationary operation at any single location. Quez Coffee Co. operates a deliberate 2–3 location rotation across Council Bluffs high-traffic sites on a recurring weekly schedule. This rotation model turns a regulatory requirement into a marketing advantage — multiple locations mean multiple customer touchpoints, greater brand visibility across the city, and reduced dependency on any single site's traffic volume.

Target operating hours: Monday–Friday 6:00 AM–2:00 PM, Saturday 7:00 AM–3:00 PM, Sunday 7:00 AM–1:00 PM. These hours capture the two peak specialty coffee windows — the morning commuter rush (6–9 AM, ~40% of daily volume) and the mid-morning/early-afternoon secondary peak (9 AM–2 PM).

Primary soft-open lot: 35th & W Broadway, Council Bluffs — a high-traffic intersection on the US-6 commuter corridor. The intersection is one of Council Bluffs' city-monitored red-light-camera locations, a published municipal indicator of high traffic volume; it sits within the established West Broadway retail density and offers natural drive-thru ingress for both directions of travel. Property owner outreach is initiated at the LLC-formation window (June–July 2026), at which point Quez Coffee Co. LLC has formal legal standing to execute a binding lot agreement. Two secondary rotation sites have been identified within the same corridor and are approached on the same timeline, so a confirmed lot is in hand well before the November 2026 build window. The mobile model preserves structural flexibility — Iowa Administrative Code 481-31 requires the unit to rotate regardless, so the business has no operational single-lot dependency.

SECTION 5 — OPERATIONS PLAN

5.1 Mobile Craft Coffee Bar — Build & Construction Strategy

The mobile craft coffee bar is owner-built under the direct supervision of Ryan Joseph Rodriquez, utilizing 12 years of mechanical expertise from his 91B MOS. A used commercial enclosed cargo trailer ($8,000, sourced via trailertrader.com or comparable) serves as the base platform. The owner's professional mechanic's toolkit and construction toolkit eliminate tool-procurement costs for the build itself.

Licensed electrical and plumbing contractors are engaged for permit-required work, operating under the owner's direct supervision. This hybrid approach — owner fabrication plus licensed trade contractors for regulatory compliance items — reduces total build cost by an estimated $15,000–$20,000 versus a fully-contracted commercial build. The unit is designed to Iowa DIAL self-contained mobile food unit specifications, eliminating commissary agreement requirements; classification will be confirmed at plan review prior to construction.

Iowa DIAL requires plan review and approval BEFORE trailer construction begins. Scaled floor drawings, equipment schedule, plumbing schematics, and finish material specifications will be submitted to DIAL Food Plan Review (515.350.7587) prior to any build activity. This is a gating requirement built into the pre-launch timeline. SBA loan funds are targeted for November 2026 disbursement, which precedes the build window.
